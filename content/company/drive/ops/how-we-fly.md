# How we fly: scheduled service, charters and shuttles

New colleagues ask this in their first week, so here it is on one page.

## Three kinds of flying

| Kind | What it is | Where it is sold | Where the numbers are |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Scheduled service | One departure per route per month on ten routes. Ten seats: 6 economy, 3 business, 1 galaxium. | The public booking site | The booking system |
| Charter rotations | Whole-ship or block bookings for corporate accounts: crew rotations, research seasons, productions. | Sales, under a contract | The CRM (deals) and Finance (invoices) |
| Shuttles | Short crew and positioning hops between Earth and Luna. | Not sold | Operations control log |

When somebody says "bookings", they mean the scheduled service. When Sales says "a deal worth 38 million", that is charter flying and it never appears in the booking system.

## The fleet

Six ships. Kestrel, Meridian and Solstice fly the inner routes. Aurora flies Mars and Jupiter. Halcyon is certified beyond Jupiter and flies Europa and Pluto. Perihelion is in refit until February 2100 and will be the second outer-system hull.

The ships and their classes are described in ops/fleet/fleet-guide.pptx, with a data sheet per class in ops/fleet/data-sheets.

## Who owns what

We fly and maintain the ships. Almost everything else belongs to somebody else.

| What | Whose it is | How we pay |
| --- | --- | --- |
| The ships | Lowell Ship Finance | A lease on each of the six, for twelve years from delivery. It runs whether the ship flies or not. |
| Building and heavy work | Hadley Yards | Built all six. Does refits and supplies parts. Routine checks are our own, at Cape Meridian. |
| The way between the ports | System Traffic Control | A charge for every flight, by distance. |
| The ports | Seven port operators | A fee for every turnaround. Our own staff handle the ships on Earth, a contracted handler elsewhere. |

The leases are the largest cost and they are fixed. That is why an empty seat is lost for good, and why a ship in refit costs twice.

## Slots, and why a ship flies about once a week

The ports give out departure slots by timetable period: March to August and September to February. We hold one scheduled slot a month on each of our ten routes. A charter or a shuttle flies on a slot asked for one at a time.

In a year that is about 120 scheduled legs, 45 charter legs and 100 shuttles: a ship flies about one leg a week. So inside Jupiter the fleet has room, and growth there is a question of demand and slots. Beyond Jupiter it is a question of hulls, because one ship is certified.

## Why the booking system starts on 10 August

Seats are sold from one sales book per timetable period. The book for September 2099 to February 2100 opened on 10 August 2099, and it is the one the booking system holds. Seats on the September and October departures that were sold before 10 August are in the closed spring book. Finance keeps the totals of closed books; the booking system does not.

The figures by year are in finance/annual/financial-summary-2094-2099.xlsx, and the company on six pages is leadership/company-profile.docx.

## What follows from this

- A route review looks at seats sold per scheduled departure (booking system) and at charter demand (CRM). They are different sources and do not add up to one number.
- Ground handlers count turnarounds, not departures. Tycho handles about fourteen of ours a month: one scheduled Earth to Moon, the rest shuttles and charters.
- A second monthly Pluto departure is a fleet question first and a sales question second, because only Halcyon can fly it today.
