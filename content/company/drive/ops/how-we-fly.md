# How we fly: scheduled service, contract flying, the Luna shuttle and the way back

New colleagues ask this in their first week, so here it is on one page.

## Four kinds of flying

| Kind | What it is | Where it is sold | Where the numbers are |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Scheduled service | One departure per route per month on ten routes. Ten seats: 6 economy, 3 business, 1 galaxium. | The public booking site | The booking system |
| Contract flying (charter rotations) | A block of seats or a whole ship for a corporate account: crew rotations, research seasons, productions. | Sales, under a contract | The CRM (deals) and Finance (invoices) |
| The Luna shuttle | Ten nights a month between Cape Meridian and Tycho. Freight in the hold; crews, staff, parts and contract travellers in the cabin. | The hold as freight, seats under contract. Not on the public site. | Operations control log |
| Positioning | The way back from where we hold no public slot, and the way out to Jupiter. | Not sold | Operations control log |

When somebody says "bookings", they mean the scheduled service. When Sales says "a deal worth 38 million", that is contract flying and it never appears in the booking system. A contract seat is never taken out of the ten public seats of a scheduled departure.

## Slots, and why we fly legs we cannot sell

A departure needs a slot, and the kind of slot decides who may be on board.

| Slot | Who may be on board | How we get one |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Public | Anyone who buys a seat | The Slot Conference of the ports and Traffic Control gives them out twice a year, for March to August and September to February. A carrier keeps a slot it flew in at least eight of ten cases. New ones appear when a port adds capacity, and they go by seats offered. |
| Contract | Travellers named under a contract with a corporate customer | Asked for one at a time. Usually given within a week. |
| Technical | Our crews, our staff, parts, and travellers we brought out ourselves | Asked for one at a time. Nobody on board may have paid for that leg. |

We entered in 2094 through the new-entrant pool: one public slot a month on each route. We hold ten today. The older carriers hold theirs from the time of the hundred-seat ships, and a slot is a departure whatever the size of the ship. That is why Orbitway can fly twice a day and we fly once a month, and why more public departures are a question for the conference before they are a question for the fleet. The next one sits at Tycho on 23 November; what we asked for is in ops/schedule/slot-requests-2100-spring.md.

Between Earth and Luna there is one more rule. The public seats belong to the Lunar Free Port's franchise carriers, Orbitway and NovaTransit. We have one public slot a month, Earth to Moon. Freight and contract seats are free of the franchise, which is what the shuttle lives on.

## The way home

Three of our ten routes end where we hold no public slot for the way back: Earth to Moon, Mars to Jupiter and Earth to Pluto. The ship has to come back anyway, on a technical slot. We may not sell that leg, but we may carry our own travellers on it. So:

| A traveller who flew with us to | Comes home | When |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Luna | On any Luna shuttle | Ten mornings a month, leaves Tycho 03:20 |
| Jupiter | On Aurora's way back to Mars | The 19th |
| Pluto | On Halcyon's way back to Earth | The 24th |
| Mars, Venus, Europa | On a scheduled departure | Bought on the site like any other seat |

The way home is in the fare. Support books it, at least 48 hours ahead, and it is confirmed when the seat is free. The same holds for the Luna shuttle in both directions: a traveller with a ticket for one of our departures rides it free to reach that departure, or to get home after it. Operations control keeps the passage list. It is not in the booking system, and it is one more spreadsheet we would like to lose.

A corporate customer whose people did not fly out with us buys the way home on its own: a reserved seat at half the outbound rate.

## The fleet

Six ships. Kestrel, Meridian and Solstice fly the inner routes. Aurora flies Mars, Jupiter and the Belt call. Halcyon is certified beyond Jupiter and flies Europa and Pluto. Perihelion is in refit at Hadley Yards, above Luna, until February 2100 and will be the second outer-system hull.

The ships and their classes are described in ops/fleet/fleet-guide.pptx, with a data sheet per class in ops/fleet/data-sheets.

## Who owns what

We fly and maintain the ships. Almost everything else belongs to somebody else.

| What | Whose it is | How we pay |
| --- | --- | --- |
| The ships | Lowell Ship Finance | A lease on each of the six, for twelve years from delivery. It runs whether the ship flies or not. |
| Building and heavy work | Hadley Yards | Built all six at its yard above Luna. Does refits and supplies parts. Routine checks are our own, at Cape Meridian and, for Aurora, at Olympus Port. |
| The way between the ports | System Traffic Control | A charge for every flight, by distance. A hop to Luna costs a fifteenth of a leg to Mars. |
| The ports | Seven port operators | A fee for every turnaround. Our own staff handle the ships on Earth, a contracted handler elsewhere. |
| The right to sell a departure | The Slot Conference | No fee. A public slot flown less than eight times in ten is lost. |

The leases are the largest cost and they are fixed. That is why an empty seat is lost for good, and why a ship in refit costs twice. What a leg costs to fly is in finance/analysis/cost-per-leg-2099.md.

## How much we fly

In 2099 that is about 117 scheduled legs, 108 contract legs, 264 legs of the Luna shuttle and 69 positioning legs: a ship flies about two legs a week, and more than half of all legs are the four hours to Luna. So inside Jupiter the fleet has room, and growth there is a question of demand and public slots. Beyond Jupiter it is a question of hulls, because one ship is certified.

Positioning is the weak point. Sixty-nine times a year a ship flies a long leg that nobody paid for, carrying a few travellers home. Every public slot we win for a way back turns one of those into a departure.

## Why the booking system starts on 10 August

Seats are sold from one sales book per timetable period. The book for September 2099 to February 2100 opened on 10 August 2099, and it is the one the booking system holds. Seats on the September and October departures that were sold before 10 August are in the closed spring book. Finance keeps the totals of closed books; the booking system does not.

The figures by year are in finance/annual/financial-summary-2094-2099.xlsx, and the company on a few pages is leadership/company-profile.docx. How we got here is leadership/our-story.md.

## What follows from this

- A route review looks at seats sold per scheduled departure (booking system) and at contract demand (CRM). They are different sources and do not add up to one number.
- Ground handlers count turnarounds, not departures. Tycho handles about fourteen of ours a month: two with a scheduled leg, ten Luna shuttles and two contract departures.
- A second monthly Pluto departure is a fleet question first and a sales question second, because only Halcyon can fly it today. It is also a slot question if its seats are to be on the public site.
- A traveller on Pluto who asks how to get home is not asking for a booking. Send them to Support.
