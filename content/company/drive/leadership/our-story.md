# How we got here

Written for the staff on 3 March 2099, five years to the day after the first departure. The figures are those of Finance; the rest is how I remember it, and Olu and Farid have corrected me where I was kind to myself.

## 2091: a ship of ten

Until the 2090s a seat to Mars was bought by contract, through an agent, weeks ahead, on a ship built for a hundred. I sold those seats at Stellar Lines for nine years. Olu flew the ships, for Orbitway. In 2091 Hadley Yards showed the Kestrel class: ten seats, a crew of three, turned round in 90 minutes. Everybody in the trade called it a yacht. Olu called me the same evening and said it was a train carriage.

## 2092: three ships and a website

We founded the company at Cape Meridian with Farid, who wrote a booking site before there was anything to book. It took its first booking nineteen months before the first ship flew. The founding round was $140 million, and most of it went to Hadley Yards as deposits on three ships.

The idea has not changed since: fly a small ship on a timetable and sell each seat the way a train ticket is sold. One price, one page, one traveller at a time.

## 2094: one ship, four routes

Kestrel flew the first departure, Earth to Mars, on 3 March 2094, with Ileana Dragomir in the left seat and four travellers behind her. We had what the Slot Conference gives a new carrier: one departure a month on each route. That year we offered 400 seats and sold 212. Revenue was $318 million and we lost 113.

One slot a month is still what we have on every route. It is the reason we are small, and the reason everything else in this company had to be good.

## 2095: nearly even

Meridian came, and Venus. Amara signed Outer Belt Logistics, our first corporate account. Perigee Capital Partners led a second round of $320 million. We lost $5 million on revenue of 809, and I thought the hard part was over.

## 2096: the fleet ahead of the network

Three ships were delivered in one year: Solstice, Aurora and Perihelion. We had ordered them when the plan said Jupiter in 2096 and the outer system in 2097, and the yard delivered on time, which nobody had planned for. We opened Jupiter, the offices on Luna and Mars, and the Luna shuttle. We had five ships and seven routes. The leases doubled to $640 million, and we lost 168.

Perihelion was the hard one. She was the first hull of her class and came without the outer-system package, because the package was not certified yet. So she could not fly the routes she was built for. She flew contracts to Jupiter and the Belt for three years, and she goes into refit this June to become what she was ordered as.

## 2097: eleven weeks

Halcyon arrived with the package, and we opened Europa. Six leases now ran at $900 million a year. The ships were seven tenths full. We lost $217 million, the most in our history.

In September Chidi showed me a sheet with eleven weeks of cash on it. I have kept it.

Two things happened. Lowell Ship Finance, who own every one of our ships, agreed to take shares instead of cash for eighteen months of the lease on Halcyon. A lessor does not do that for a customer it expects to lose. And Perigee led a third round of $460 million, which closed in December. Ingrid joined us the week it was being negotiated and has not let me forget what the term sheet said: no new route in 2098, and fill the ships you have.

Lowell holds 24% of the company today, Perigee 40%, and the three of us and the staff 36%.

## 2098: no new route

We did what the term sheet said. No new route, no new ship. Seats sold went from 672 to 832, the ships from 70% to 77% full. Revenue was $2,652 million, and for the first time there was a profit: 122 million.

## 2099

Pluto opens in April, on Halcyon, once a month. It is the first route we add with money we earned.

What I would like you to take from this: we are the smallest of four, and we will stay small in seats as long as a slot counts the same for a ship of ten and a ship of a hundred. So we cannot afford a traveller who was told one price and charged another, or a departure that leaves a day late. The others can. We cannot.

Renata
