A scheduled carrier with six leased ships of ten seats. It sells seats to travellers on 10 routes from Earth to Pluto and flies charter rotations for companies. It flies and maintains the ships; it does not build them and does not own the ports.

Until the 2090s a seat to Mars was bought by contract, through an agent, weeks ahead, on a ship built for a hundred. In 2091 Hadley Yards showed the Kestrel class: ten seats, a crew of three, turned round in 90 minutes. Galaxium was founded the next year to fly it on a timetable and sell each seat the way a train ticket is sold: one price, one page, one traveller at a time.

What it depends on

Almost everything except the flying, the maintenance and the selling belongs to somebody else.

The shipsLowell Ship Finance

All six are leased, for twelve years from delivery. The lease runs whether a ship flies or not. The largest cost we have.

The way between the portsSystem Traffic Control

A charge for every flight, by distance, paid from a prepaid account.

Building and heavy workHadley Yards

Built all six at its yard in orbit above Luna. Does refits and supplies parts. Routine checks are our own, at Cape Meridian.

How a year of flying is made up

The ports give out departure slots at the Slot Conference, twice a year, for the timetable periods March to August and September to February. A public slot is the right to sell seats to the public on one departure, and we hold one a month on each of our ten routes. Contract and technical slots are asked for one at a time. A ship flies about two legs a week, and more than half of all legs are the four hours to Luna. So inside Jupiter the fleet has room: growth there is a question of demand and public slots. Beyond Jupiter it is a question of hulls, because one ship is certified.

Why the booking system starts on 10 August

Seats are sold from one sales book per timetable period. The book for September 2099 to February 2100 opened on 10 August 2099, and it is the one the booking system holds. Seats on the September and October departures that were sold before 10 August are in the closed spring book. Finance keeps the totals of closed books; the booking system does not.

How a traveller gets home

A traveller who flies out with us to Luna, Jupiter or Pluto flies home with us on the same fare. We may not sell the way back, because we hold no public slot for it, but we may carry our own travellers on a leg we fly anyway. Booked through Support at least 48 hours ahead, at no charge, and confirmed when the seat is free. The passage list is kept by operations control and is not in the booking system.

Where it stands

The smallest of the four scheduled carriers, and the only one that sells a single seat to Pluto on a public site at a price that holds. About 190,000 seats were sold between the planets in 2098, by the count of Interplanetary Travel Weekly. We sold 832 of them. We are not in the business of carrying most people: we carry the ones for whom a ship of ten, a fixed price and a seat booked in minutes is worth it.

OrbitwayThe largest on the inner routes: 24 ships of 80 to 120 seats, flying since 2071. Sells frequency.

We: Fly beyond Jupiter. They do not.

NovaTransitLow fares on Earth, Luna and Mars: 15 ships of 160 seats, flying since 2084. Holds the Lunar Free Port's franchise for public seats with Orbitway.

We: Fly beyond Mars, and keep to the schedule.

Stellar LinesThe widest outer-system network: 11 ships of 40 to 60 seats, flying since 2068, Pluto twice a month. 10% to 20% above our fares.

We: Cost less, and are booked in minutes instead of by contract.
